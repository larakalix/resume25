## Art direction

The street reference was visually inspected in the live viewer. These are implementation choices drawn from its broad visual language, not claims about historical reconstruction:

- Use a compact mix of two-storey houses and shops, with varied roof heights rather than one repeated silhouette.
- Give domestic facades a restrained mix of blue, pink, peach and green. Keep timber and cream plaster as a common material vocabulary so the quarter fits the existing world.
- Make roof courses visible through overlapping tile shapes and broad eaves. Small chimney or vent shapes add scale without needing dense texture maps.
- Layer wood lattice windows, sliding shopfronts, awnings or noren, potted plants and modest utility details. Keep primary signs legible, with meaningful English names instead of arbitrary Japanese glyphs.
- Keep lanes narrow and inviting while leaving actual walking clearance. Overhead wires, utility poles and a sequence of small frontages can frame the route; a quieter courtyard landmark provides a contrasting open space.

The reference also places a turquoise canal and small timber bridge between the houses. That is useful context for a possible later water lane, rather than a requirement to duplicate this composition or introduce an untested water crossing in this pass.
